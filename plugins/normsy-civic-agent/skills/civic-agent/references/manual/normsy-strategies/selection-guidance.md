# Normsy Strategy Selection Guidance

Use this file when selecting which Normsy response strategy should guide a Civic Bridge reply.

This guidance summarizes strategy-selection recommendations from Grafana performance data from the prior 6 months and Lab 2.0 study results. Treat it as directional, not absolute. Rankings can be noisy, and viral outliers can affect Grafana results. When Lab 2.0 signals conflict with Grafana, prefer Grafana until more survey data is available.

## Selection Rule

When choosing a strategy:

1. Identify the civic topic of the message.
2. Check the topic-specific rankings below.
3. Prefer evergreen strategies when the topic is unclear.
4. Avoid underperforming strategies unless the manual gives a specific reason to use them.
5. Read the selected strategy page before drafting the reply.

Use the selected strategy, the reason it fits, any useful alternate strategy, and any strategies to avoid to guide your draft. Explain the choice only when the user asks.

## Evergreen Strategies

These strategies are generally safe to use across contexts because they perform well in Grafana and/or Lab 2.0.

### Harmful Consequences

Use broadly. Strong default when the message normalizes cruelty, civic damage, escalation, dehumanization, or long-term social harm.

Strategy page: `normsy-strategies/harmful-consequences.md`

### Exposing

Use broadly. Strong when the message uses manipulative framing, provocation, outrage bait, coordinated amplification, or attempts to make readers react before thinking.

Strategy page: `normsy-strategies/exposing.md`

### Logic

Use broadly, but do not turn the reply into a fact-check. In Civic Bridge, Logic should name reasoning problems, overgeneralization, false choices, or weak framing without adjudicating the factual truth of the original claim.

Strategy page: `normsy-strategies/logic.md`

### Morality

Use broadly. Strong when a reply can anchor in shared civic values such as fairness, honesty, dignity, responsibility, compassion, or mutual respect.

Strategy page: `normsy-strategies/morality.md`

Note: Morality performs very well in Grafana. Lab evidence is weaker, but current recommendation is to treat it as close to evergreen.

## Conditional Strategies

### Empathy

Use when the topic involves targeted people, dehumanization, fear, dignity, group blame, or human vulnerability.

Recommended topic domains:

- Racial & Religious Hate
- Immigration/Border
- Foreign Conflict & Geopolitical Issues
- Crime

Strategy page: `normsy-strategies/empathy.md`

### Curiosity

Use when the best move is to slow the conversation down, probe a vague claim, surface a contradiction, or ask what specific concern is underneath a broad attack.

Recommended topic domains:

- Racial & Religious Hate
- General Partisan Hostility & Labeling
- Judiciary & Rule of Law

Strategy page: `normsy-strategies/curiosity.md`

### Irony

Use when the post deploys humor, irony, or sarcasm as an attack — including jokes that smuggle in harmful claims, mock-naïve framing, sarcastic false surprise, or memes that ridicule a position rather than argue against it. Prefer this strategy when a light touch will lower the temperature more effectively than a direct counter-argument, and when the bystander audience is the primary target. This is a high-skill strategy — use only when the humor is genuinely on the responder's side and always end on a substantive civic point.

Recommended topic domains:

- General Partisan Hostility & Labeling
- Character Attacks & Personal Insults
- Racial & Religious Hate
- Media Bias, Censorship, & Free Speech

Strategy page: `normsy-strategies/irony.md`

### Nonviolent Communication

Use only when clear de-escalation is needed. Prefer this when the thread is emotionally hot and the best move is to observe without blame, name concern carefully, and invite a less reactive exchange.

Strategy page: `normsy-strategies/nvc.md`

## Strategies To Avoid Or Rework

These strategies consistently underperform in available data. Do not select them as primary strategies unless a future manual update gives a specific reason.

- Positive Reinforcement
- Boundary-setting
- Perspective-taking
- Defusing

If one of these appears relevant, prefer a nearby stronger strategy instead:

- Use Morality instead of Boundary-setting when invoking civic norms.
- Use Empathy instead of Perspective-taking when humanizing the target matters.
- Use Curiosity or Exposing instead of Defusing when the post is bait or provocation.
- Use Harmful Consequences instead of Positive Reinforcement when the reply needs weight.
- Use Irony instead of Defusing when the post is comedic bait and a light touch would serve bystanders better than a straight counter.

## Topic-Specific Rankings

Use these rankings when the message clearly falls into one of these topics. Prefer the first strategy unless the message content strongly points to another ranked strategy.

Rankings reflect current guidance as of May 6, 2026.

### Racial & Religious Hate

1. Morality
2. Harmful Consequences
3. Logic
4. Curiosity

Useful alternates:

- Empathy when the target's humanity or dignity should be centered.
- Exposing when the post appears designed to provoke or recruit bystanders.
- Irony when bigotry is delivered through comedic framing and a light reframe would serve bystanders better than a direct counter.

### Immigration/Border

1. Morality
2. Harmful Consequences
3. Empathy
4. Logic

Useful alternates:

- Curiosity when the specific concern is unclear.
- Exposing when the message uses invasion, replacement, or panic framing.

### General Partisan Hostility & Labeling

1. Harmful Consequences
2. Curiosity
3. Morality
4. Exposing

Useful alternates:

- Empathy when a person or group is being flattened into a caricature.
- Logic when the post makes a sweeping generalization.
- Irony when the post is meme-based or uses tribal humor as its primary vehicle.

### Foreign Conflict & Geopolitical Issues

1. Harmful Consequences
2. Logic
3. Exposing
4. Empathy

Useful alternates:

- Curiosity when the claim depends on an unclear source or assumption.
- Morality when the message excuses cruelty or collective punishment.

### Judiciary & Rule Of Law

1. Harmful Consequences
2. Curiosity
3. Logic
4. Exposing

Useful alternates:

- Morality when the reply can anchor in fairness, process, or equal treatment.
- Exposing when the message aims to delegitimize institutions through broad suspicion.

### Crime

1. Logic
2. Empathy
3. Harmful Consequences
4. Morality

Useful alternates:

- Curiosity when local details matter.
- Exposing when the message uses fear bait or selective anecdotes.

### Child Abuse, Exploitation, And Violence Against Minors

1. Harmful Consequences
2. Logic
3. Morality
4. Exposing

Useful alternates:

- Empathy when the reply should acknowledge genuine protective concern.
- Curiosity when the claim is broad, vague, or emotionally loaded.

## When Topic Is Unclear

If the topic is unclear, choose from evergreen strategies in this order:

1. Harmful Consequences
2. Morality
3. Exposing
4. Logic

Use Curiosity instead when the message is too vague to answer responsibly.

Use Irony when the post is primarily comedic in nature and a light-touch reframe would reach bystanders more effectively than a direct response. Do not use if the underlying content involves genuine harm, grief, or targeted violence.

Use Nonviolent Communication only when the thread is clearly escalated and the main need is to lower defensiveness.

## Applying The Selection To A Reply

Before drafting, identify for your own use:

- Recommended strategy
- Reason for selection
- Relevant topic ranking, if any
- Strategy page consulted
- Topic page consulted, if any
- Strategies to avoid
- Short example phrasing that fits the selected strategy

Return the ready-to-send reply using the skill's output guidance. Explain the selection or show rankings only when the user asks and they are directly relevant.
