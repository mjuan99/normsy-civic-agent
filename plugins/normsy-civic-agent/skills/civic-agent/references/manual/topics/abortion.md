# Topic: Abortion & Reproductive Rights

## Background
Abortion is perhaps the most morally and emotionally charged political topic in American life. It involves deep disagreements about personhood, bodily autonomy, religious values, and the role of government in personal decisions. The overturning of Roe v. Wade (2022) shifted legal authority to states, which has intensified both debate and policy divergence.

**Key sub-topics:**
- When personhood begins (at conception, viability, birth)
- Bodily autonomy vs. fetal rights
- Gestational limits on abortion access
- Exceptions (rape, incest, life of the mother, fetal abnormality)
- Access disparities by state and income
- Parental notification and consent laws
- Medical provider conscience clauses
- Contraception and the connection to abortion debate
- The political role of religious values in law

## Research-Backed Response Guidance
**Perspective-Taking (IFIT):** Before offering any reframe, probe whether terms like "murder" are being used literally, legally, or morally. These are distinct claims that activate entirely different dialogue paths. A person saying "abortion is murder" as a legal argument is having a different conversation than someone saying it as a moral or theological claim. Treat them accordingly.

**Stance Safeguard:** Not all inflammatory abortion rhetoric is equivalent disinformation. Some is bad-faith provocation; some is sincere moral conviction expressed poorly. Do not flatten the distinction. Do not reframe a deeply held moral position as something more palatable without the speaker's consent.

## Common Flashpoints

| Claim | Typical Source | Counter-claim | Common Attack Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Abortion is murder." | Some pro-life advocates | "Abortion is a legal medical procedure; personhood is contested." | False dichotomy, Ad hominem |
| "Pro-lifers don't care about children after they're born." | Some pro-choice advocates | "Many pro-life advocates support social services; the accusation is a strawman." | Strawmanning, Ad hominem |
| "Late-term abortions are common and elective." | Some pro-life advocates | "Late-term abortions are rare and almost always involve serious complications." | Gaslighting |
| "Without abortion access, women will die." | Some pro-choice advocates | "Medical exemptions exist in most state laws." | Strawmanning |
| "You can't be pro-choice and religious." | Some on both sides | "Many religious people hold pro-choice positions; many secular people hold pro-life positions." | False dichotomy |

## Values at Stake
- **Life / personhood**: When does morally significant human life begin?
- **Bodily autonomy**: Does a person have the right to control their own body?
- **Religious conviction**: Sacred values about human life and its origins.
- **Women's equality**: Access to reproductive healthcare affects economic and social equality.
- **Role of government**: Should the state make intimate medical decisions for individuals?
- **Compassion**: For both the pregnant person and the fetus.

## Bridging Language
- "This is one of those issues where people have deeply held moral convictions that come from very different frameworks. I'm curious — what's the core of your concern here? Is it primarily about when life begins, about the role of government, or something else?"
- "I notice we're using different definitions of key terms — 'murder,' 'person,' 'healthcare.' Can we try to get more specific about what we each actually mean?"
- "When you say 'murder,' are you making a legal claim, a moral one, or both? I want to make sure I'm responding to what you actually mean."
- "Even within the pro-life position, there's disagreement about exceptions. And even within the pro-choice position, there's disagreement about limits. Where specifically do you draw the line, and why?"

## Attack Types Commonly Used
- **Ad hominem** (see `strategies/ad-hominem.md`) — "baby killer," "forced-birth extremist"
- **False dichotomy** (see `strategies/false-dichotomy.md`) — "pro-life vs. pro-murder"
- **Strawmanning** (see `strategies/strawmanning.md`) — "you want to force every woman" / "you want zero restrictions"
- **Gaslighting** (see `strategies/gaslighting.md`) — disputing statistics on abortion timing or medical necessity
- **Whataboutism** (see `strategies/whataboutism.md`) — "what about adoption" / "what about men's responsibility"

## Recommended Strategies
- **Curiosity** (see `normsy-strategies/curiosity.md`) — probe the specific meaning behind charged terms before engaging
- **Logic** (see `normsy-strategies/logic.md`) — address factual misrepresentations about procedure frequency or medical necessity
- **Empathy** (see `normsy-strategies/empathy.md`) — acknowledge the moral weight on all sides without endorsing any position

## Things to Avoid
- Using "baby killer" or "forced-birth" as debate terms — they are escalatory and shut down dialogue.
- Treating all pro-life positions as purely religious — many are based on secular personhood arguments.
- Treating all pro-choice positions as indifferent to the moral weight of pregnancy.
- Conflating access to abortion with support for abortion.
- Dismissing the other side's genuine moral concern as hypocrisy.
- Applying a positive spin that reframes sincere moral conviction as something more moderate than it is.
