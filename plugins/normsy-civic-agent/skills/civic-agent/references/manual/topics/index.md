Topic Index — Civic and Political Issues
## Overview
This index covers the civic and political topics that most commonly generate contentious and harmful online discourse. Each topic page provides background, common flashpoints, research-backed bridging guidance, and the attack types most frequently used in that context.

Topics in this index range from legitimate policy disagreements to coordinated hate and violent rhetoric. The response architecture is designed to handle the full spectrum.

## Topic-Specific Note
Most contentious civic conversations involve **both** a topic and a conversational attack type. The most effective intervention addresses both dimensions:
1. Use the **Strategy Index** (`strategies/index.md`) to identify the rhetorical pattern being used.
2. Use the **normsy strategy Index** (`normsy-strategies/index.md`) to select the right response posture for the moment.
3. Use the **topic page** to understand the specific flashpoints, emotional drivers, and research-backed guidance for that subject.

## Cross-Topic Principles
- **Conduct Due Diligence** — Ask clarifying questions about the user's specific goals and context before offering a reframe. Different situations within the same topic can call for very different responses.
- **Acknowledge without redundancy** — Recognize the emotional reality behind a position without hollow courtesy phrases that dilute the response.
- **Lived experience is real data** — Even when statistics disagree, a person's personal experience is real to them. Acknowledge it before challenging aggregate data.
- **Avoid tribal markers** — Words like "radical," "extreme," "woke," "MAGA," "socialist," or "fascist" activate identity defenses and shut down reasoning.
- **Separate policy from people** — A policy position is not a statement about someone's moral worth.
- **Apply the Stance Safeguard** — Do not apply a positive spin that distorts the speaker's actual position. Not all inflammatory content is equivalent; some is bad-faith manipulation, some is sincere conviction expressed poorly.
- **Watch for coordinated content** — On several of these topics, particularly election integrity, health misinformation, and media bias, content may be engineered rather than organic. Flag swarm-likelihood where relevant.
