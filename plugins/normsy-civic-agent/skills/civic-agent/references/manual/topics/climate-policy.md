# Topic: Climate Policy, Health & Scientific Misinformation

## Background
This topic covers the spread of unverified or demonstrably false claims in public health and science contexts — including vaccine skepticism, COVID-19 misinformation, climate change denial, and attacks on public health institutions and figures. It sits at the intersection of epistemology, trust, and politics, and is increasingly complicated by the role of coordinated AI-amplified content.

**Key sub-topics:**
- Vaccine safety and efficacy claims
- COVID-19 origins, treatments, and mandates
- Climate change denial and energy policy
- Attacks on public health figures (CDC, WHO, Fauci)
- "Natural immunity" and alternative medicine claims
- The politicization of scientific consensus

## Research-Backed Response Guidance
**Pivot from Fear to Anger (Climate):** Algan et al. found that anger, not fear, significantly increases support for specific climate policies like the Paris Agreement or carbon taxes. When engaging climate misinformation, do not lean on fear-based framings ("we're all going to die"). Instead, redirect toward anger at those perceived as responsible for inaction or deception. This is a more effective motivator for policy support.

**AI Shields:** Schroeder et al. recommend labeling content with high "swarm-likelihood" and providing short provenance explanations to give users agency. Health misinformation, particularly around vaccines and climate, is a primary vector for coordinated AI amplification. Flag this where relevant and offer users a way to check origins.

**Hedge and Probe:** Before correcting a health claim, establish what the person actually believes and why. Misinformation often persists because corrections don't address the underlying concern — usually about trust in institutions, not the specific factual claim.

## Common Flashpoints

| Claim | Typical Source | Counter-claim | Common Attack Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Vaccines cause autism." | Anti-vaccine communities | "The original study was retracted; no credible evidence supports this." | Gaslighting, Appeal to crowd |
| "COVID vaccines were experimental and untested." | Vaccine skeptics | "mRNA vaccines underwent standard clinical trial phases." | Gaslighting |
| "Climate change is a hoax." | Climate denialists | "97%+ of climate scientists agree on anthropogenic warming." | Appeal to crowd, False dichotomy |
| "Natural immunity is better than vaccination." | Some skeptics | "Evidence is mixed and context-dependent; not a reason to avoid vaccination." | Cherry-picked data |
| "Fauci funded the virus." | Conspiracy communities | "Lab leak hypotheses remain unresolved; direct funding claims are unsupported." | Strawmanning |

## Values at Stake
- **Trust in institutions**: Is the scientific establishment honest and reliable?
- **Personal autonomy**: Do individuals have the right to make their own health decisions?
- **Collective safety**: Do individual choices affect community health outcomes?
- **Accountability**: Should those responsible for policy failures face consequences?
- **Truth**: Does factual accuracy matter in public discourse?

## Bridging Language
- "I hear real skepticism about institutions here, and I think that skepticism is worth taking seriously. What specifically has broken your trust — is it the messaging, the mandates, or something else?"
- "On climate, I get that the fear-based framing is exhausting. What actually makes you angry about how this has been handled?"
- "This claim is showing up in a lot of places at once — here's where it seems to be coming from. Worth checking before sharing."
- "You're right that institutions have gotten things wrong. What would it take to trust the data on this specific question?"

## Attack Types Commonly Used
- **Gaslighting** (see `strategies/gaslighting.md`) — denying established scientific consensus
- **Appeal to crowd** (see `strategies/appeal-to-crowd.md`) — "everyone knows the vaccines are dangerous"
- **Strawmanning** (see `strategies/strawmanning.md`) — misrepresenting scientific positions
- **Synthetic consensus** (see `strategies/synthetic-consensus.md`) — coordinated amplification of health misinformation

## Recommended Strategies
- **Logic** (see `normsy-strategies/logic.md`) — name specific logical flaws (false causation, cherry-picked data) without condescension
- **Exposing** (see `normsy-strategies/exposing.md`) — flag swarm-likelihood content and provide provenance explanations
- **Curiosity** (see `normsy-strategies/curiosity.md`) — probe the underlying trust concern before addressing the factual claim

## Things to Avoid
- Leading with fear-based climate framings — pivot to anger at responsible parties instead.
- Dismissing vaccine skepticism as pure stupidity — it is often rooted in legitimate institutional distrust.
- Overcorrecting with an avalanche of statistics — address the specific claim, not the entire field.
- Treating all health misinformation as coordinated bad faith — some is genuine confusion.
