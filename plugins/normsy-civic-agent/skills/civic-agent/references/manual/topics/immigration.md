# Topic: Immigration & Border

## Background
Immigration is one of the most emotionally and politically charged topics in contemporary American discourse. It involves genuine policy disagreements about border security, legal immigration pathways, asylum processes, and the treatment of undocumented people — layered over deep cultural anxieties about identity, belonging, and economic competition.

**Key sub-topics:**
- Border security and wall construction
- Undocumented immigration and deportation policy
- Asylum processes and refugee admissions
- DACA and pathways to citizenship
- Framing migrants as criminals or a national security threat
- Sanctuary city policies
- Economic impacts of immigration
- Cultural and demographic change narratives

## Research-Backed Response Guidance
**Address Emotional Drivers:** Research identifies anger, not just policy disagreement, as a primary driver of support for restrictive immigration policies. Acknowledge the emotional reality before engaging the factual claims. Attempting to correct misinformation before acknowledging the underlying anger is likely to fail.

**Conduct Due Diligence:** Ask clarifying questions about the user's specific concerns before reframing. "Immigration" covers an enormous range of distinct issues. A person concerned about fentanyl trafficking is having a different conversation than someone concerned about labor market competition or cultural change. Probe which issue is actually at stake.

**Stance Safeguard:** Do not reframe calls for enforcement as "caring about the rule of law" if the speaker is expressing something more visceral. Do not sanitize the position to make it more palatable. Engage with what is actually being said.

## Common Flashpoints

| Claim | Typical Source | Counter-claim | Common Attack Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Migrants are criminals / invaders." | Restrictionist rhetoric | "Immigrants commit crimes at lower rates than native-born citizens." | Ad hominem, Gaslighting |
| "They're taking our jobs." | Economic anxiety framing | "Evidence on labor market impacts is mixed and sector-specific." | False dichotomy, Strawmanning |
| "Open borders are the goal." | Anti-immigration rhetoric | "No mainstream political figure advocates for open borders." | Strawmanning |
| "Mass deportation is the only solution." | Hard-line restrictionists | "Enforcement-only approaches have not resolved undocumented population issues historically." | False dichotomy |
| "Sanctuary cities protect criminals." | Restrictionist rhetoric | "Sanctuary policies limit local cooperation with federal immigration enforcement; crime data is contested." | Gaslighting |

## Values at Stake
- **Safety**: Is the community protected from crime and external threat?
- **Rule of law**: Should immigration laws be enforced consistently?
- **Dignity**: Are migrants treated as human beings regardless of legal status?
- **Fairness**: Is the system equitable for both legal immigrants and citizens?
- **Economic security**: Are wages and employment protected?
- **Compassion**: How should we treat people fleeing danger or poverty?

## Bridging Language
- "I hear real anger here. Before I respond to the specifics, help me understand what's driving it — is this primarily about safety, about fairness to people who came legally, about something else?"
- "What specifically are you most concerned about — the border itself, the people crossing, the policies in place, or something else? I want to address the right thing."
- "Most people across the political spectrum agree the immigration system is broken. Where do you think the fix needs to start?"
- "The data on [specific claim] is actually more complicated than it looks. Want to dig into it?"

## Attack Types Commonly Used
- **Ad hominem** (see `strategies/ad-hominem.md`) — dehumanizing language about migrants
- **Strawmanning** (see `strategies/strawmanning.md`) — "open borders" misrepresentation
- **False dichotomy** (see `strategies/false-dichotomy.md`) — "either mass deportation or open borders"
- **Gaslighting** (see `strategies/gaslighting.md`) — disputing crime statistics or economic data
- **Appeal to crowd** (see `strategies/appeal-to-crowd.md`) — "everyone knows migrants are dangerous"

## Recommended Strategies
- **Curiosity** (see `normsy-strategies/curiosity.md`) — probe which specific concern is driving the rhetoric before responding
- **Empathy** (see `normsy-strategies/empathy.md`) — acknowledge the anger and the humanity of both the speaker and the migrants
- **Logic** (see `normsy-strategies/logic.md`) — address false causation and overgeneralization in crime and economic claims

## Things to Avoid
- Correcting statistics before acknowledging the emotional driver — it will not land.
- Using "undocumented" vs. "illegal" as a political signal — pick a consistent term and explain it if asked.
- Treating all restrictionist positions as racist — many are rooted in genuine rule-of-law concerns.
- Treating all pro-immigration positions as indifferent to security — most are not.
- Reframing dehumanizing language as "just strong feelings" without naming what it is.
