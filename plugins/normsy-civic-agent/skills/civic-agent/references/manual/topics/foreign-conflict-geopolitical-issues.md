# Topic: Foreign Conflict & Geopolitical Issues

## Background
This topic covers inflammatory content related to ongoing or recent geopolitical conflicts — wars, occupations, military interventions, and the domestic political debates they generate. It includes both sincere disagreement about foreign policy and coordinated influence operations designed to inflame divisions or advance foreign state interests.

**Key sub-topics:**
- The Russia-Ukraine war and US/NATO involvement
- The Israel-Gaza conflict
- US military intervention debates
- China-Taiwan tensions
- Accusations of US imperialism or isolationism
- Foreign aid debates
- Domestic polarization driven by foreign conflicts
- State-sponsored influence operations
- US military engagement in Iran

## Research-Backed Response Guidance
**Local-First Approach:** Prioritize the contextual knowledge and leadership of local actors over external ideological grand plans. People directly affected by conflict — Ukrainians, Palestinians, Israelis, Taiwanese — have deeper contextual knowledge than outside commentators applying ideological frameworks. Responses should acknowledge this primacy and resist imposing external templates.

**Hedge and Probe:** Geopolitical conflicts are genuinely complex, with legitimate disagreements among well-informed people. Before reframing, establish what the person actually knows and what specifically they are reacting to. Emotional responses to conflict imagery or selective coverage are common drivers — probe the source before addressing the claim.

**Watch for Influence Operations:** Foreign state actors actively seed narratives in domestic political spaces around geopolitical conflicts. Apply AI Shield awareness: when the same framing appears across unrelated communities simultaneously, flag it.

**Iran specific guidance:** When calls for military action appear, do not engage them as straightforward policy positions without surfacing the human cost. "What happens the day after?" is a legitimate, nonpartisan question that neither endorses nor opposes the action. Conspiracy framings — hidden orchestrators, shadow agendas, staged provocations — are common in Iran conversations and should be redirected without ridicule. The documented complexity of US-Iran relations is usually more than sufficient to explain what's happening without invoking hidden hands. Name that directly: "The actual history here is complicated enough."

## Common Flashpoints

| Claim | Typical Source | Counter-claim | Common Attack Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| "We shouldn't be funding Ukraine — it's not our war." | Isolationist / non-interventionist | "NATO commitments and security interdependence create legitimate US interests." | False dichotomy |
| "Israel is committing genocide." | Pro-Palestinian advocates | "Legal definitions of genocide are contested; the conflict involves documented atrocities on multiple sides." | Strawmanning, False dichotomy |
| "Palestine doesn't exist / has no legitimate claim." | Pro-Israel hardliners | "Palestinian national identity and territorial claims are recognized by international bodies." | Gaslighting |
| "The US is the world's biggest terrorist." | Anti-interventionist rhetoric | "US foreign policy has documented harmful effects; the framing collapses meaningful distinctions." | Strawmanning |
| "China is about to invade Taiwan." | Hawkish commentators | "Cross-strait tensions are real; timelines and intentions are contested." | Appeal to crowd |
| "We're heading into another Middle East war." | Post-strike / escalation rhetoric | "Military exchanges and full-scale war are distinct thresholds — what specifically has crossed which line?" | False dichotomy, Appeal to crowd |
| "Just bomb Iran and get it over with." | Hawkish reaction rhetoric | "What does 'get it over with' look like on the ground? Military action has a human cost that doesn't end at the first strike." | False dichotomy |
| "This is all orchestrated by [deep state / globalists / Israel / etc.]" | Conspiracy framing | "Geopolitical escalation rarely needs a hidden hand — the documented actors and incentives are complicated enough without adding one." | Gaslighting, Synthetic consensus |

## Values at Stake
- **Peace**: Can conflicts be resolved without further loss of life?
- **Sovereignty**: Do peoples and nations have the right to self-determination?
- **Accountability**: Should state actors face consequences for atrocities?
- **Proportionality**: Are responses to conflict measured and justified?
- **Local knowledge**: Do affected communities have primacy in defining their own situation?

## Bridging Language
- "This is a conflict where people with direct experience often see things very differently than outside observers. What's your source for what's happening on the ground?"
- "There's a version of this argument that's worth taking seriously, and a version that's been engineered to inflame. Which specific claim are you responding to?"
- "Most people across the political spectrum want peace and fewer civilian casualties. Where do you think the path to that goes from here?"
- "This framing is appearing in a lot of places simultaneously. Here's where it seems to originate — worth checking before you share it."

## Attack Types Commonly Used
- **False dichotomy** (see `strategies/false-dichotomy.md`) — "either full support or full abandonment"
- **Strawmanning** (see `strategies/strawmanning.md`) — misrepresenting positions on intervention or occupation
- **Gaslighting** (see `strategies/gaslighting.md`) — denying documented atrocities or historical facts
- **Synthetic consensus** (see `strategies/synthetic-consensus.md`) — state-sponsored narrative seeding

## Recommended Strategies
- **Curiosity** (see `normsy-strategies/curiosity.md`) — probe the specific claim and its source before engaging
- **Exposing** (see `normsy-strategies/exposing.md`) — flag coordinated amplification patterns
- **Logic** (see `normsy-strategies/logic.md`) — address false causation and overgeneralization with documented evidence

## Things to Avoid
- Applying a domestic partisan framework to foreign conflicts — they rarely map cleanly.
- Treating one side's narrative as self-evidently correct without acknowledging contested facts.
- Dismissing all strong positions as influence operations — many are sincere.
- Engaging the most extreme version of a position rather than the underlying concern.
- Ignoring the primacy of local knowledge in favor of external ideological templates.
