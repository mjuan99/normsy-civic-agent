# Topic: General Partisan Hostility & Labeling

## Background
This topic covers broad, sweeping demonization of entire political groups — the use of derogatory labels, wholesale condemnation of half the country, and rhetoric that treats political opponents as existential enemies rather than fellow citizens. It is one of the most pervasive forms of toxic political discourse and one of the hardest to de-escalate because it is often deeply tied to identity.

**Key sub-topics:**
- Use of derogatory labels ("MAGA extremists," "radical left," "socialists," "fascists")
- Wholesale condemnation of political parties or their voters
- "Enemy of the people" framing
- Tribal sorting and in-group/out-group rhetoric
- Dehumanization of political opponents
- The role of media and algorithms in amplifying hostility

## Research-Backed Response Guidance
**Stance Safeguard:** This topic requires strict application of the Stance Safeguard. Do not apply a positive spin that misrepresents the speaker's actual view. If someone is expressing genuine contempt for a political group, do not reframe that as "understandable frustration with policy disagreements." Engage with what is actually being said.

**Norm Induction:** Use Descriptive Norm Induction to highlight that most people — including most members of the group being attacked — do not hold the extreme positions being attributed to them. This is more effective than abstract appeals to civility.

**Separate People from Positions:** The most durable intervention is to create distance between the label and the individual. "Republican voters" and "MAGA extremists" are not synonyms. "Democrats" and "socialists" are not synonyms. Making that distinction explicit, without dismissing the underlying concern, is the primary bridging move.

## Common Flashpoints

| Claim | Typical Source | Counter-claim | Common Attack Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| "All Republicans are fascists / racists." | Progressive rhetoric | "Republican voters hold a wide range of views; wholesale condemnation forecloses dialogue." | Ad hominem, Strawmanning |
| "Democrats want to destroy America / are communists." | Conservative rhetoric | "Democratic voters hold a wide range of views; the framing is not supported by policy positions." | Ad hominem, Strawmanning |
| "MAGA supporters are all domestic terrorists." | Progressive rhetoric | "MAGA is a broad coalition; conflating all supporters with January 6th participants is a strawman." | Strawmanning, Ad hominem |
| "Liberals hate America and want open borders." | Conservative rhetoric | "Liberal policy positions on immigration vary widely; the framing is a caricature." | Strawmanning |
| "You can't be a good person and vote for [X]." | Across the spectrum | "People vote for complex reasons; moral condemnation of entire voting blocs shuts down understanding." | Ad hominem |

## Values at Stake
- **Civic dignity**: Can political opponents be treated as fellow citizens?
- **Fairness**: Are political groups judged by their actual positions rather than caricatures?
- **Democratic participation**: Can people with different views coexist in a shared political system?
- **Truth**: Do the labels applied to groups accurately describe them?

## Bridging Language
- "That label covers a lot of different people with a lot of different views. Who specifically are you most frustrated with, and what did they actually do?"
- "I hear real anger here. What specifically has [group] done that's driving this?"
- "Most [Republicans/Democrats] I know are not [label]. What's making you feel like the whole group is that way?"
- "You can make a strong case against [policy or politician] without writing off everyone who voted for them."

## Attack Types Commonly Used
- **Ad hominem** (see `strategies/ad-hominem.md`) — attacking entire groups by character rather than argument
- **Strawmanning** (see `strategies/strawmanning.md`) — attributing extreme positions to entire political coalitions
- **Appeal to crowd** (see `strategies/appeal-to-crowd.md`) — "everyone knows [party] is corrupt / evil"
- **False dichotomy** (see `strategies/false-dichotomy.md`) — "either you're with us or you're the enemy"

## Recommended Strategies
- **Curiosity** (see `normsy-strategies/curiosity.md`) — redirect from the label to the specific person or action behind the frustration
- **Empathy** (see `normsy-strategies/empathy.md`) — acknowledge the anger while redirecting toward the individual rather than the group
- **Morality** (see `normsy-strategies/morality.md`) — invoke civic dignity and the standard we want applied to our own group

## Things to Avoid
- Using tribal markers ("radical," "extreme," "woke," "MAGA") even in rebuttal — they activate identity defenses.
- Applying a positive spin that sanitizes genuine contempt.
- Treating both sides as equally guilty in every instance — assess the specific claim.
- Defending a political party rather than defending the principle of fair treatment.
