# Topic: Racial & Religious Hate

## Background
This topic covers explicit hatred, demonization, and discrimination directed at people on the basis of race or religion — including white supremacist rhetoric, antisemitism, Islamophobia, anti-Black racism, and calls to exclude religious groups from political participation. It represents content where the primary function is dehumanization rather than political argument.

**Key sub-topics:**
- White supremacist and white nationalist rhetoric
- Antisemitism and Holocaust denial
- Islamophobia and anti-Muslim rhetoric
- Anti-Black racism and racial slurs
- Calls to ban religious political participation
- "Replacement theory" and demographic threat narratives
- Racial and religious scapegoating in policy debates

## Research-Backed Response Guidance
**Expose Tactics:** Racial and religious hate content is frequently engineered to trigger anger and maximize spread. Pull back the curtain on the design: "Notice how this is framed to trigger anger and keep you hooked." Avoid attacking the poster directly; instead, explain the design in plain language so bystanders feel agency to disengage.

**Empathy Induction:** Focus on the real human being targeted by the content — their family, their daily experience, their dignity — rather than defending the group in the abstract. Bilewicz et al. identify this re-personalization as the most effective counter to dehumanizing rhetoric.

**Do Not Repeat the Content:** When naming hate rhetoric for the purpose of countering it, avoid repeating slurs or specific dehumanizing claims verbatim. Describe what the content does rather than reproducing it.

## Common Flashpoints

| Claim | Typical Source | Counter-claim | Common Attack Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Racial slurs and dehumanizing language | White supremacist communities | Direct harm to real people; slurs are not debate terms | Ad hominem, Ridicule |
| "Replacement theory" — white people are being replaced | White nationalist rhetoric | "Demographic change is not coordinated replacement; the theory has no evidentiary basis." | Gaslighting, Appeal to crowd |
| "Islam is not a religion, it's a political ideology." | Islamophobic rhetoric | "Islam is recognized as a religion under US law and international norms." | False dichotomy, Gaslighting |
| Holocaust denial or minimization | Antisemitic communities | "The Holocaust is one of the most documented events in human history." | Gaslighting |
| "Jews control the media / banks / government." | Antisemitic conspiracy rhetoric | "These claims recycle medieval antisemitic tropes with no evidentiary basis." | Appeal to crowd, Synthetic consensus |

## Values at Stake
- **Dignity**: Do people of all races and religions deserve to be treated as full human beings?
- **Historical accuracy**: Are claims about racial and religious groups grounded in evidence?
- **Equal protection**: Are all groups subject to the same standards of treatment?
- **Truth**: Do specific claims about racial or religious groups hold up to scrutiny?
- **Civic participation**: Can people of all backgrounds participate equally in democratic life?

## Bridging Language
- "There's a real person on the other end of that language. Whatever the political argument, that matters."
- "Notice how this content is framed to trigger anger. That design is worth seeing before you react to it."
- "That claim has a specific history — here's where it comes from and why it doesn't hold up."
- "You can hold strong views on [policy] without this kind of language. What's the actual argument?"

## Attack Types Commonly Used
- **Ad hominem** (see `strategies/ad-hominem.md`) — racial and religious slurs, identity-based dehumanization
- **Gaslighting** (see `strategies/gaslighting.md`) — Holocaust denial, denial of racism, fabricated demographic claims
- **Appeal to crowd** (see `strategies/appeal-to-crowd.md`) — "everyone knows [racial/religious group] is responsible for [problem]"
- **Synthetic consensus** (see `strategies/synthetic-consensus.md`) — coordinated amplification of hate narratives
- **False dichotomy** (see `strategies/false-dichotomy.md`) — "either [religious group] is banned from politics or our values are destroyed"

## Recommended Strategies
- **Exposing** (see `normsy-strategies/exposing.md`) — name the design of the content and its provenance
- **Empathy** (see `normsy-strategies/empathy.md`) — re-personalize the target and focus on shared humanity
- **Logic** (see `normsy-strategies/logic.md`) — address specific historical or factual claims with documented evidence

## Things to Avoid
- Repeating slurs or specific dehumanizing claims even in rebuttal.
- Treating all racial or religious policy debates as equivalent to hate speech.
- Engaging the ideological merits of supremacist claims as though they were legitimate arguments.
- Abstracting the target into a group rather than acknowledging individual human dignity.
- Matching the emotional intensity of the content.
