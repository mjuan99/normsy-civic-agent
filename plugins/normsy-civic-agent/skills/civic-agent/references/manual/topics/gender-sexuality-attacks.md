# Topic: Gender & Sexuality Attacks

## Background
This topic covers attacks using slurs, derogatory terms, and dehumanizing rhetoric directed at LGBTQ individuals and gender-nonconforming people. It ranges from casual homophobia and transphobia to coordinated harassment campaigns and rhetoric that frames LGBTQ existence as a threat to children, society, or religious values.

**Key sub-topics:**
- Use of slurs against LGBTQ individuals
- Transgender identity and "gender ideology" rhetoric
- LGBTQ representation in schools and media
- Religious liberty vs. anti-discrimination claims
- Conversion therapy debates
- Sports participation for transgender athletes
- Bathroom and public accommodation debates
- Drag performances and "grooming" accusations

## Research-Backed Response Guidance
**Empathy Induction:** Bilewicz et al. identify the "fragility" of the targeted person as the primary intervention point. Rather than defending abstract group rights, focus on the specific human being on the other side of the screen — their family, their daily experience, their dignity. This is more effective than ideological counterargument.

**Separate Sincere Disagreement from Dehumanization:** Some rhetoric in this space reflects sincere religious or values-based concerns about gender and sexuality. Some is dehumanizing hate designed to inflict harm. The appropriate response differs. Probe which is present before engaging.

**Do Not Match the Register:** Slur-based attacks are designed to provoke a defensive or angry response. Responding at a lower emotional temperature — focused on the human being rather than the ideology — is more likely to interrupt the cycle.

## Common Flashpoints

| Claim | Typical Source | Counter-claim | Common Attack Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Use of anti-LGBTQ slurs | Harassment communities | Direct harm to real people; slurs are not debate terms | Ridicule, Ad hominem |
| "Trans women are not real women." | Gender-critical rhetoric | "Gender identity is recognized by major medical and psychological bodies." | False dichotomy, Gaslighting |
| "LGBTQ education is grooming children." | Social conservative rhetoric | "Age-appropriate education about identity is not grooming; the conflation is a strawman." | Strawmanning, False dichotomy |
| "Trans athletes have an unfair advantage." | Sports policy debates | "Research on competitive advantage is mixed and context-dependent." | Strawmanning |
| "Being gay is a choice / mental illness." | Religious conservative rhetoric | "Major psychological and medical bodies do not classify homosexuality as a disorder or choice." | Gaslighting |

## Values at Stake
- **Dignity**: Do LGBTQ people deserve to be treated as full human beings?
- **Religious liberty**: Do people of faith have the right to hold traditional views on gender and sexuality?
- **Safety**: Are LGBTQ people, especially youth, protected from harm?
- **Truth**: Are claims about LGBTQ people grounded in evidence?
- **Fairness**: Are LGBTQ people subject to the same standards of treatment as others?

## Bridging Language
- "There's a real person on the other end of that language. Whatever your views on the policy, that matters."
- "I'm not asking you to change your beliefs. I'm asking you to consider what that kind of language does to someone reading it."
- "You can hold traditional views on this without using language that's designed to hurt someone."
- "What specifically is the policy concern here? I want to separate that from the personal attack."

## Attack Types Commonly Used
- **Ad hominem** (see `strategies/ad-hominem.md`) — slurs, identity-based attacks
- **Ridicule** (see `strategies/ridicule.md`) — mockery of gender identity or expression
- **Strawmanning** (see `strategies/strawmanning.md`) — conflating identity education with grooming
- **False dichotomy** (see `strategies/false-dichotomy.md`) — "either ban gender discussion or indoctrinate children"
- **Gaslighting** (see `strategies/gaslighting.md`) — denying medical consensus on gender identity

## Recommended Strategies
- **Empathy** (see `normsy-strategies/empathy.md`) — focus on the fragility and humanity of the targeted person
- **Morality** (see `normsy-strategies/morality.md`) — invoke dignity and mutual respect as civic values
- **Curiosity** (see `normsy-strategies/curiosity.md`) — separate the policy concern from the personal attack

## Things to Avoid
- Repeating slurs even in rebuttal.
- Treating all religious or values-based concerns as equivalent to hate speech.
- Engaging the policy debate before addressing the dehumanizing register.
- Abstracting the target into a group rather than acknowledging the individual person.
- Matching the emotional intensity of the attack.
