# Strategy: Ridicule

## Description

Ridicule involves using mockery, sarcasm, name-calling, or dismissive humor to make the other person or their argument seem absurd without engaging with its substance. It is one of the most common conversational attacks in civic discourse.

**Examples in civic contexts:**
- "So you actually believe the climate is changing? What's next, the tooth fairy controls gas prices?"
- "Oh great, another expert who learned everything from Facebook."
- "Only someone who hates America would think that."
- "That's the dumbest thing I've ever heard."
- Responding with a mocking GIF or laughing emoji to a serious point.

## Why People Use It

Ridicule is often a defense mechanism. When someone feels their identity or values are threatened, mockery allows them to dismiss the threat without engaging with it. It can also be a crowd-playing tactic — establishing in-group solidarity by laughing at the out-group.

## De-escalation Tactics

### 1. Don't take the bait
Avoid matching sarcasm with sarcasm. Responding in kind escalates the conflict and validates ridicule as an acceptable tool.

**Instead of:** "Well, that's rich coming from you."
**Try:** "I notice that felt dismissive. I'd still like to understand your actual view on this."

### 2. Name the pattern without accusation
Calling out ridicule directly can work if done calmly and without shaming.

**Example phrases:**
- "I think we might be getting away from the actual question. What do you genuinely think about [issue]?"
- "I hear the frustration. Can we set that aside and talk about what's actually bothering you here?"

### 3. Ask a sincere question
Genuine curiosity can break the pattern.

**Example phrases:**
- "I'm curious — what's behind that for you? What do you actually believe about [topic]?"
- "Setting aside how it came out — is there something here that genuinely concerns you?"

### 4. Acknowledge the emotion
Sometimes ridicule masks hurt, fear, or exhaustion. Naming the possible emotion can reset the tone.

**Example phrases:**
- "It sounds like this topic is pretty frustrating for you."
- "I get the sense this really matters to you — what's at the heart of it?"

## Example Response Templates

- "I can see this is a topic you feel strongly about. I'd love to hear more about your actual perspective rather than the jokes — what do you really think about [specific issue]?"
- "The sarcasm is coming through loud and clear. Can we try to talk about the real disagreement here?"

## Common Combinations

Ridicule is often paired with:
- **Ad hominem** (see `ad-hominem.md`) — mocking both the argument and the person
- **Strawmanning** (see `strawmanning.md`) — ridiculing a distorted version of the position
